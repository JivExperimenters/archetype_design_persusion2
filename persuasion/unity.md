# Unity

[Home](../README.md) · [Liking](liking.md) · Brennan Dahmen

## Mechanism

Unity appeals to a shared identity: people see themselves as part of the same “we.” It goes beyond having similar tastes. Cialdini's [Influence at Work overview](https://www.influenceatwork.com/7-principles-of-persuasion/) distinguishes this sense of belonging from simply liking another person.

## When and how to use it

Use this when the audience already has a meaningful bond, such as a campus community, an established study group, or a couple's shared life. Name that connection and show how the offer supports something the group does together. Pronouns alone are insufficient: adding “our” to an unrelated product does not demonstrate a shared identity.

A real campaign should accurately identify the community it serves and avoid pretending the brand is a member of a group it has no relationship with. The examples here are fictional concepts with those relationships specified in their briefs.

## Example: Side Quest

- **Audience:** Existing college study groups.
- **Headline:** Our study group. Our silly ritual.
- **Supporting copy:** Turn your group's study break into a shared game. Keep the inside jokes going.
- **Offer:** Free printable party-game kit.
- **CTA:** Get the game kit.
- **Destination:** A proposed direct download of the printable kit, without an account requirement. The PDF product is outside the static-hero assignment.
- **Why the mechanism is present:** The request is framed around an existing group's shared ritual and inside jokes. The product gives that group another way to spend time together.

See [Jester heroes 3 and 4](../archetypes/jester.md#hero-3-de-stijl--unity). The same kit and action appear in both; style changes do not change what the audience receives.

## The other applications

| Brand | Existing bond | Visible cue |
|---|---|---|
| [Common Table](../archetypes/everyperson.md) | Students at the same college | “Our campus. Our table.” and an invitation to make room for another student. |
| [Little Ritual](../archetypes/lover.md) | A couple's relationship | “The two of us” and a reference to the story they share. |

The card example applies the principle to an existing pair, rather than asserting a relationship between a stranger and the brand. This is a design interpretation, not proof of a persuasive effect.

## Misuse to avoid

Do not imply that buying is required to be a “real” member of a group. Avoid outsiders-versus-insiders language, fabricated membership, or pressure such as “people like us never refuse.” Shared identity should invite participation without threatening belonging. The same green button or friendly photograph could appear in a Liking design; the explicit existing bond is what distinguishes these versions.
