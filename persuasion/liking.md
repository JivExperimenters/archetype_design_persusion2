# Liking

[Home](../README.md) · [Unity](unity.md) · Brennan Dahmen

## Mechanism

Liking can make a request more persuasive when the audience feels positive toward the person or brand making it. Genuine similarities, appreciation, and cooperation can help establish that connection. Cialdini's [Influence at Work overview](https://www.influenceatwork.com/7-principles-of-persuasion/) identifies these factors; a pleasant color palette alone does not establish the mechanism.

## When and how to use it

Use this when a visitor needs to feel comfortable approaching a host, service, or unfamiliar activity. Identify a real shared preference and show it through the brand's behavior or voice. A beginner-friendly host should actually welcome beginners and explain the activity patiently.

For the fictional brands in this project, shared interests are part of the creative brief. Before a real launch, the hosts or creators would need to confirm that the statements describe them accurately. No invented testimonials or popularity counts are necessary.

## Example: Common Table

- **Audience:** College students who are new to board games.
- **Headline:** New to game night? So are we.
- **Supporting copy:** Meet friendly hosts who love easy games and low-pressure evenings.
- **Offer:** Free beginner board-game night.
- **CTA:** Reserve my seat.
- **Destination:** A proposed reservation form with a session choice and confirmation. This assignment contains static mockups, not a working registration system.
- **Why the mechanism is present:** The hosts present themselves as peers who share the visitor's experience and preferences. The invitation lowers the social distance between host and newcomer.

See [Everyperson heroes 1 and 2](../archetypes/everyperson.md#hero-1-bauhaus--liking). The two styles use identical copy, so the persuasion claim stays consistent even when the look changes.

## The other applications

| Brand | Visible similarity | How it helps the request |
|---|---|---|
| [Side Quest](../archetypes/jester.md) | A playful voice and shared enjoyment of bad puns | The visitor can recognize a compatible sense of humor before downloading the game kit. |
| [Little Ritual](../archetypes/lover.md) | The brand explicitly shares a liking for tea and slow conversation | The cards feel made by people who understand the desired kind of evening. |

These are reasoned design applications, not measured evidence that a visitor will convert.

## Misuse to avoid

Do not invent a shared background, write fake compliments, or use stereotypes to imply that everyone in a group likes the same things. Friendly imagery should match the experience after the click. Liking also differs from Unity: enjoying the same games is a similarity; belonging to the same campus community or an existing study group is a shared identity.
