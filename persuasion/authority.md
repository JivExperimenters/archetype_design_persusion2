## What is it?
Authority is a persuasion principle: people may give more weight to a recommendation when it comes from a credible, relevant expert or institution. The communicator should make the basis of that credibility visible. A title alone does not prove that a person is qualified to speak on every subject.

## How do you recognize or use it?
Authority can be supported by relevant credentials, experience, research, professional standards, or a clearly identified institution. Use the signal that actually fits the claim. Let the reader follow a path from a relevant person or institution to verifiable qualifications, supporting evidence, and a transparent recommendation.

Before publishing, ask whether the expertise matches the specific claim, whether readers can check the credential or evidence, whether the evidence supports the strength of the wording, and whether payment, employment, sponsorship, or another material relationship is disclosed. Keep the claim, attribution, source, and disclosure together. Never invent a person, credential, endorsement, result, or institutional approval.

## Examples
### Innocent archetype application

Archetype: [Innocent](../archetypes/innocent.md)  
Headline: A simple drink. A clear label.  
CTA: "Explore PurePath" invites the reader to explore the fictional PurePath drink concept.  
Mechanism: The hero reserves a visible place for a verified expert review and source. The reassuring Innocent language and orderly layout keep the claim easy to inspect, while the placeholder prevents the design from implying that an expert has already endorsed the concept. Authority would work only after relevant, verifiable evidence replaces that warning.

### Teaching wireframe

![Sample endorsement card with placeholders for an expert's name, relevant credentials, quotation, evidence link, and disclosure.](../assets/tutorial/authority-endorsement-card.svg)

This is a teaching wireframe only. It contains placeholders, not a real expert, endorsement, credential, or product claim. A responsible endorsement card includes the expert's real name and relevant verified qualifications, a faithful quotation with context, a direct link to supporting research or the review method, and a clear disclosure of material connections.
