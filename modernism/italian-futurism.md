# Italian Futurism

[Home](../README.md) · Brennan Dahmen

## What it is

Italian Futurism made printed language feel active: words could suggest noise, acceleration, and collision instead of following a quiet paragraph. It belongs to the early modernist avant-garde, although its expressive disruption differs from the later orderly modernism of Swiss typography. Marinetti's experimental poetry and Depero's mechanically bound portfolio show two approaches: the page as a performance and the book as a designed object. The examples below document those approaches through original works rather than modern imitations.

## Recognize and apply it

| Feature | What it does | How I would use it |
|---|---|---|
| Changing type size, weight, and direction | Makes words act like loud and quiet sounds | Enlarge one action word and angle a secondary phrase; keep essential offer copy level. |
| Diagonals and compressed geometric shapes | Creates forward movement and visual tension | Point an illustrated wedge toward the product, leaving the button on a calm background. |
| Typography treated as an image | Gives the title a physical presence | Build one expressive headline composition instead of decorating every sentence. |

This approach suits an energetic cultural event or experimental publication. It is less suitable for a long instruction page where competing reading directions would slow the reader down. I would adapt its visual methods without reproducing the celebration of combat suggested by Marinetti's wartime subject.

## Historical examples

### F. T. Marinetti — Zang Tumb Tumb, 1914

- **Creator and work:** Filippo Tommaso Marinetti, *Zang Tumb Tumb: Adrianopoli Ottobre 1912: Parole in Libertà*, 1914. MoMA credits the typography to Cesare Cavanna and the publisher as Edizioni Futuriste di “Poesia,” Milan. The assignment's “Zang Tumb Tuuum” refers to this work; the title here follows the museum catalog.
- **Institution and direct source:** [Museum of Modern Art, object 31450](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/31450).

**What I observe:** The heavy central words hold the cover together while smaller sound words move diagonally toward the bottom. The changing sizes make the text appear to recede. The limited black-and-ochre palette leaves the typography responsible for the movement.

**Hero adaptation:** I would place an oversized action word beside a smaller repeated phrase that trails toward the product image. The CTA would stay horizontal and isolated so the visual experiment does not hide the action.

### Fortunato Depero — Depero Futurista (Bolted Book), 1927

- **Creator and work:** Fortunato Depero, *Depero Futurista*, 1927; published by Dinamo-Azari, Milan. The book gathers Depero's work and is physically held together by bolts.
- **Direct sources:** [Cooper Union: Return of the Bolted Book](https://cooper.edu/humanities/news/return-bolted-book) and [Bolted Book project fact sheet](https://www.boltedbook.com/fact-sheet/).

**What I observe:** The two visible bolts make the binding part of the identity. Large black letters interlock with sharp white shapes, creating an engineered, compressed composition. The cover acts like a demonstration of the maker's skill rather than a neutral label.

**Hero adaptation:** I would combine a bold angular title with a close view of the product's construction. One repeated circular detail could suggest the bolts without copying the cover. A neutral strip below the composition would contain the offer and button.
