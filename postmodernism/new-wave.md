# New Wave Typography

[Home](../README.md) · Brennan Dahmen

## What it is

New Wave typography grew from experiments that challenged the uniform spacing, stable grid, and restrained hierarchy associated with Swiss modernist graphic design. Wolfgang Weingart's Basel teaching helped spread these experiments; April Greiman extended them through electronic imagery and layered compositions. It is commonly discussed as part of the move toward postmodern graphic design. [Cooper Hewitt's New Wave history](https://exhibitions.cooperhewitt.org/willismith/williwear-new-wave-graphics/) supports that context.

New Wave is not simply “random text.” Its strongest layouts establish a path through deliberately disrupted alignments and overlapping materials.

## Recognize and apply it

| Feature | Effect | Hero use |
|---|---|---|
| Broken alignment and unexpected spacing | Challenges a single predictable reading path | Offset the headline and one annotation while keeping the CTA fixed. |
| Layered type, photographs, and graphic marks | Suggests several kinds of information happening together | Combine a product image with a diagram or short contextual label. |
| Visible halftones, pixels, and reproduction texture | Makes the production process part of the visual language | Use texture in the illustration, leaving body copy on a clean panel. |

This style can suit a music, media, or design event where experimentation is part of the offer. It needs restraint when the audience must quickly understand a price, deadline, or instruction.

## Historical examples

### April Greiman — Does It Make Sense?, 1986

- **Creator and work:** April Greiman, *Does It Make Sense?*, 1986, photolithograph; published by Walker Art Center and MIT Press as *Design Quarterly* 133.
- **Institution and source:** [LACMA, object 194922](https://collections.lacma.org/object/194922). Also see [Greiman's project record](https://www.aprilgreiman.com/does-it-make-sense) and [her MoMA audio discussion](https://www.moma.org/audio/playlist/297/4835).

**What I observe:** The horizontal figure organizes a field of diagrams, numbers, small images, and changing pixel textures. The body is both the subject and a surface across which information is layered. Blue ties together elements that otherwise differ greatly in scale.

**Hero adaptation:** I would use one large product silhouette as an anchor, then layer two small annotations around it. I would borrow the relationship between a large image and fine detail rather than reproduce the figure or its crowded information density.

### Wolfgang Weingart — Kunstkredit Basel 1976/77, 1977

- **Creator and work:** Wolfgang Weingart, *Kunstkredit Basel 1976/77*, 1977, offset lithograph. The 1976/77 wording is in the title; the exhibition dates printed on the poster are in May 1977.
- **Institution and source:** [LACMA, object 207622](https://collections.lacma.org/object/207622).

**What I observe:** White and black text alternates across strips and halftone fields. Photographic fragments collide with abstract shapes, while the exhibition date remains bold enough to find. The image demonstrates that a monochrome layout can be complex without relying on bright colors.

**Hero adaptation:** I would layer an image crop and a large typographic strip, then give the offer its own contrasting panel. The texture would stop before the button and small copy, preserving a clear final action.
