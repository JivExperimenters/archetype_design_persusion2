# Push Pin Style
[Back to this section](README.md) · [Home](../README.md)

## What is it?
Push Pin Style emerged in the United States in the 1960s and early 1970s, especially through the work of Push Pin Studios. Founded by Milton Glaser, Seymour Chwast, Reynold Ruffins, and Edward Sorel, the studio became a major force in postmodern illustration and graphic design. The style combined hand-drawn qualities, collage techniques, and references to older print traditions, creating a visual language that felt witty, expressive, and full of personality.

This approach stood apart from strictly modernist design because it embraced illustration as a serious design tool. The studio used thick lines, playful composition, and layered references from Victorian engraving, old posters, woodcuts, comics, and roadside signage. The result was a design language that felt graphic and highly memorable without losing clarity.

## How do you recognize or use it?
Push Pin Style is recognizable through loose, hand-rendered illustration, collage, strong outlines, and a mix of historical references. Common features include bold, playful color choices, organic forms, and a deliberate sense of improvisation. The layouts often feel carefully composed but not rigid, mixing visual references in a way that still reads clearly.

This style works well for editorial design, branding, posters, and cultural projects that need warmth, humor, and visual personality. It is especially effective when a brand wants to feel approachable and creative without looking overly corporate.

### How to use it in a hero
Use a layered collage or illustration-based hero image, combining hand-drawn shapes with a simple typographic block. Keep the headline memorable and concise, and use a few carefully chosen color accents so the composition feels lively but controlled.

## Examples
### Milton Glaser, *I Love New York* (1977)
This poster is one of the most famous examples of the era's graphic energy. It blends a bold, iconic typographic treatment with a simple but memorable visual statement. The work shows how an illustration can be both highly effective and emotionally direct.

### Push Pin Studio, poster and editorial work
Push Pin Studio's posters and magazine work often mixed a variety of historical references with a contemporary sensibility. Their compositions used strong illustration and playful treatment of type to create modern images that remained rooted in print culture.
