# Jester
[Back to this section](README.md) · [Home](../README.md) · Brennan Dahmen

## What is it?
The Jester seeks enjoyment, playful connection, and relief from excessive seriousness.

## How do you recognize or use it?
A light voice, surprising objects, and a harmless visual joke can make a short break feel inviting. Use it when play is part of the actual experience. Avoid jokes at the audience's expense or humor that hides what the product does.

## Examples
Both designs use the same fictional brand, SIDE QUEST, offering a free printable party-game kit for college study groups. These are static mockups.

### Example 1: Modernist
<img src="../assets/archetype/Jester/jester-hero-1.png" alt="Jester hero 1: De Stijl treatment for SIDE QUEST, with the headline Serious about silly games. and a Get the game kit button" width="640">

Archetype: Jester
Style: De Stijl — [Piet Mondrian, Composition in Red, Blue, and Yellow (MoMA)](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/80160)
Persuasion: [Liking](../persuasion/liking.md)
Headline: Serious about silly games.

Supporting copy: For friends who love bad puns and quick laughs. Take a playful study break.

CTA: Get the game kit — downloads the free kit, with no account or purchase required.

The bent smiling pencil and cheerful game pieces make the Jester's playfulness visible. Liking is invited through the brand's compatible sense of humor and the audience's enjoyment of bad puns. Mondrian-inspired rectangular zones organize the joke without making the action difficult to locate.
